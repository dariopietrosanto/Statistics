Executive Summary & Research Directives

In high-throughput data systems, network intrusion monitoring, and statistical computing, datasets frequently arrive as unbounded continuous streams. Traditional textbook formulas for the sample variance require either keeping the entire dataset in memory ($O(n)$ space across two passes) or computing differences of large sums ($\sum x^2 - (\sum x)^2/n$), which suffers from catastrophic cancellation in IEEE 754 floating-point arithmetic.

This assignment is structured into two core mathematical and empirical investigations:

  • Exercise 1 – Mathematical Derivation of Welford's Recurrence Formulas: Following the algebraic framework of Knuth’s recurrence for the running mean, we derive the exact single-pass update rule for the sum of squared differences ($M_2$ or $SS$) and the sample variance ($s^2$). We demonstrate the 3-line core algorithmic implementation and prove its numerical immunity to floating-point cancellation.
  • Exercise 2 – Penetration Scoring, Sampling Distributions & Inferential Statistics: Extending the hacker penetration model, $m$ concurrent hackers attack $n$ enterprise systems, receiving an I.I.D. penetration score $X_i \in [0, 10]$ from a chosen probability distribution (with theoretical mean $\mu$ and variance $\sigma^2$). Using Welford's algorithm on the fly, each hacker computes their trajectory's sampling mean $\bar{X}$ and sampling variance $S^2$. We compare the grand ensemble averages across all $m$ hackers with $\mu$ and $\sigma^2$, analyze the fundamental role of Bessel’s correction ($n-1$), and investigate how system scale $n$ governs the variability decay $O(1/n)$ of sample estimators in inferential statistics.

Exercise 1: Derivation of Knuth's and Welford’s Recurrence Formulas

1. Knuth’s Recurrence Formula for the Running Mean

Let $x_1, x_2, \dots, x_k$ denote a streaming sequence of observations. The sample mean at step $k$ is defined by:

$$\bar{x}_k = \frac{1}{k} \sum_{i=1}^k x_i$$

To derive an online recurrence that expresses $\bar{x}_k$ purely in terms of the prior mean $\bar{x}_{k-1}$ and the incoming element $x_k$, we isolate the $k$-th term from the summation:

$$\bar{x}_k = \frac{1}{k} \left( \sum_{i=1}^{k-1} x_i + x_k \right) = \frac{1}{k} \left( (k-1)\bar{x}_{k-1} + x_k \right)$$

Rewriting $(k-1)\bar{x}_{k-1} = k\bar{x}_{k-1} - \bar{x}_{k-1}$:

$$\bar{x}_k = \frac{k\bar{x}_{k-1} - \bar{x}_{k-1} + x_k}{k} = \bar{x}_{k-1} + \frac{x_k - \bar{x}_{k-1}}{k}$$

Defining the innovation (or forecast error) as $\delta_k = x_k - \bar{x}_{k-1}$, we obtain Knuth's online mean recurrence:

$$\mathbf{\bar{x}_k = \bar{x}_{k-1} + \frac{\delta_k}{k}}$$

2. Welford’s Recurrence Formula for the Sum of Squares ($M_2$ or $SS$)

The sum of squared differences from the sample mean at step $k$ is defined as:

$$M_{2, k} = \sum_{i=1}^k (x_i - \bar{x}_k)^2$$

We wish to derive an update rule $M_{2, k} = M_{2, k-1} + \Delta M_2$. Notice that each deviation from the new mean $\bar{x}_k$ can be expressed in terms of the old mean $\bar{x}_{k-1}$:

$$x_i - \bar{x}_k = (x_i - \bar{x}_{k-1}) - (\bar{x}_k - \bar{x}_{k-1}) = (x_i - \bar{x}_{k-1}) - \frac{\delta_k}{k}$$

Squaring this deviation:

$$(x_i - \bar{x}_k)^2 = (x_i - \bar{x}_{k-1})^2 - 2 \frac{\delta_k}{k} (x_i - \bar{x}_{k-1}) + \frac{\delta_k^2}{k^2}$$

Now sum this expression over the first $k-1$ historical elements ($i = 1, \dots, k-1$):

$$\sum_{i=1}^{k-1} (x_i - \bar{x}_k)^2 = \sum_{i=1}^{k-1} (x_i - \bar{x}_{k-1})^2 - 2 \frac{\delta_k}{k} \underbrace{\sum_{i=1}^{k-1} (x_i - \bar{x}_{k-1})}_{=\, 0} + (k-1) \frac{\delta_k^2}{k^2}$$

Because the sum of deviations around a sample mean is identically zero by definition ($\sum_{i=1}^{k-1} (x_i - \bar{x}_{k-1}) = 0$), the cross-product term completely vanishes! Thus:

$$\sum_{i=1}^{k-1} (x_i - \bar{x}_k)^2 = M_{2, k-1} + \frac{k-1}{k^2} \delta_k^2$$

Next, consider the final $k$-th term $(x_k - \bar{x}_k)^2$:

$$x_k - \bar{x}_k = x_k - \left( \bar{x}_{k-1} + \frac{\delta_k}{k} \right) = (x_k - \bar{x}_{k-1}) - \frac{\delta_k}{k} = \delta_k - \frac{\delta_k}{k} = \delta_k \left( \frac{k-1}{k} \right)$$

Squaring this term yields:

$$(x_k - \bar{x}_k)^2 = \delta_k^2 \frac{(k-1)^2}{k^2}$$

Adding both portions together gives the full sum $M_{2, k}$:

$$M_{2, k} = \sum_{i=1}^{k-1} (x_i - \bar{x}_k)^2 + (x_k - \bar{x}_k)^2 = M_{2, k-1} + \frac{k-1}{k^2} \delta_k^2 + \frac{(k-1)^2}{k^2} \delta_k^2$$

Factoring out $\frac{k-1}{k^2} \delta_k^2$:

$$M_{2, k} = M_{2, k-1} + \frac{k-1}{k^2} \delta_k^2 \cdot \left[ 1 + (k - 1) \right] = M_{2, k-1} + \frac{k-1}{k^2} \delta_k^2 \cdot k = M_{2, k-1} + \frac{k-1}{k} \delta_k^2$$

Observing that $(x_k - \bar{x}_k) = \delta_k \left( \frac{k-1}{k} \right)$, we substitute back to obtain Welford's elegant recurrence:

$$\mathbf{M_{2, k} = M_{2, k-1} + \delta_k \cdot (x_k - \bar{x}_k) = M_{2, k-1} + (x_k - \bar{x}_{k-1})(x_k - \bar{x}_k)}$$

From $M_{2, k}$, the unbiased sample variance and population variance are computed directly:

$$s_k^2 = \frac{M_{2, k}}{k - 1} \quad (k \ge 2), \qquad \sigma_k^2 = \frac{M_{2, k}}{k}$$

3. The 3-Line Core Code Implementation

As required by the assignment, the core update step inside any loop over observations requires exactly 3 lines:

// Core 3-line recurrence inside a stream loop for each observation x at step k: const delta = x - mean; mean += delta / k; M2 += delta * (x - mean); // (x - mean) automatically evaluates (x - new_mean)

Theoretical Extension: Online Higher-Order Moments (Skewness & Kurtosis)

In 2007, Timothy B. Terriberry and Donald Knuth demonstrated that Welford’s single-pass philosophy extends analytically to the third ($M_3$) and fourth ($M_4$) central moments:

$$\delta_k = x_k - \bar{x}_{k-1}, \quad \bar{x}_k = \bar{x}_{k-1} + \frac{\delta_k}{k}, \quad \delta_k^* = x_k - \bar{x}_k$$ $$M_{3, k} = M_{3, k-1} + \delta_k \delta_k^* (\delta_k - \delta_k^*) - 3 \frac{\delta_k}{k} M_{2, k-1}$$ $$M_{4, k} = M_{4, k-1} + \delta_k \delta_k^* (\delta_k^2 - \delta_k \delta_k^* + (\delta_k^*)^2) + 6 \frac{\delta_k^2}{k^2} M_{2, k-1} - 4 \frac{\delta_k}{k} M_{3, k-1}$$

From these sums, one computes streaming Sample Skewness $g_1 = \frac{\sqrt{k} M_{3, k}}{M_{2, k}^{3/2}}$ and Sample Kurtosis $g_2 = \frac{k M_{4, k}}{M_{2, k}^2} - 3$ in a single streaming pass using strict $O(1)$ memory!

3. Impact of System Depth $n$ on the Variability of Sample Means and Variances

How does increasing the number of systems attempted by each hacker affect estimation uncertainty? This batch experiment simulates ensembles across $n \in [5, 10, 20, 50, 100, 250, 500, 1000]$ to demonstrate the fundamental $O(1/n)$ decay of estimator variability:

Theoretical Analysis of Variability Scaling:

  • Variability of the Sampling Means ($\text{Var}(\bar{X}) = \sigma^2/n$): The sample mean has variance directly inversely proportional to $n$. Its standard error $\text{SE}(\bar{X}) = \sigma/\sqrt{n}$ contracts at rate $O(1/\sqrt{n})$, compressing the distribution of hacker performance estimates into a razor-sharp Dirac spike at $\mu$.
  • Variability of the Sampling Variances ($\text{Var}(S^2) \approx \frac{2\sigma^4}{n-1} + \frac{\kappa \sigma^4}{n}$): When $n$ is small ($n = 5$), individual hackers estimate wildly dispersed sample variances (ranging from near zero to more than double $\sigma^2$). As $n$ reaches enterprise scales ($n \ge 250$), $\text{Var}(S^2)$ collapses towards zero. Every single hacker’s individually estimated variance converges to the exact true parameter $\sigma^2$!

Theoretical Synthesis & Answers to Key Inferential Questions

1. What conclusions can you draw from comparing the grand averages with $\mu$ and $\sigma^2$?

The empirical simulations reveal two foundational theorems of statistical theory:

  1. Unbiasedness of the Sample Mean ($\mathbb{E}[\bar{X}] = \mu$): Because expectation is a linear operator: $$\mathbb{E}[\bar{X}] = \mathbb{E}\left[\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^n X_i\right] = \frac{1}{n} \sum_{i=1}^n \mathbb{E}[X_i] = \frac{1}{n} (n\mu) = \mu$$ The grand average across $m$ hackers $\overline{\bar{X}} = \frac{1}{m}\sum_{j=1}^m \bar{X}_j$ converges to $\mu$ by the Law of Large Numbers as $m \to \infty$.
  2. Unbiasedness of the Sample Variance ($\mathbb{E}[S^2] = \sigma^2$) and Bessel’s Correction: The sample variance uses the sample mean $\bar{X}$ rather than the true unknown population mean $\mu$. Because the sample points are inevitably closer to their own sample mean $\bar{X}$ than to the true mean $\mu$ (since $\bar{X}$ minimizes the sum of squared deviations), dividing by $n$ introduces a systematic downward bias of $\frac{n-1}{n}\sigma^2$: $$\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{1}{n} \sum_{i=1}^n (X_i - \bar{X})^2\right] = \frac{n-1}{n} \sigma^2$$ By dividing by the degrees of freedom $n - 1$ instead of $n$ (Bessel's correction), the estimator becomes strictly unbiased: $$\mathbb{E}[S^2] = \mathbb{E}\left[\frac{1}{n-1} M_2\right] = \frac{1}{n-1} \cdot (n-1)\sigma^2 = \sigma^2$$

2. Why are these results useful in inferential statistics?

In observational data science and cybersecurity forensics, the true latent parameters $\mu$ (the true baseline security vulnerability rate) and $\sigma^2$ (the variance of system resilience) are never known. We are only given a single hacker's audit log or a finite telemetry sample of size $n$.

  • Absence of Systematic Error: Because $\bar{X}$ and $S^2$ are proven to be unbiased estimators, security analysts can substitute sample statistics into decision algorithms without compounding systematic over- or under-estimation errors.
  • Confidence Intervals & Student’s $t$-Distribution: When population variance $\sigma^2$ is unknown, replacing $\sigma$ with the unbiased sample standard deviation $S$ allows constructing exact confidence intervals via the $t$-statistic: $$t = \frac{\bar{X} - \mu}{S / \sqrt{n}} \sim t_{n-1}$$ This enables calculating rigorous bounds: e.g., "We are 95% confident that the mean exploit depth lies between $\bar{X} \pm t_{0.025} \frac{S}{\sqrt{n}}$."
  • Hypothesis Testing for Security Interventions: Unbiased estimators enable two-sample Welch $t$-tests to evaluate whether deploying a zero-trust patch or web application firewall statistically significantly reduced penetration scores ($H_0: \mu_{\text{before}} = \mu_{\text{after}}$).
  • Real-time Streaming Anomaly Detection: Welford's algorithm allows security operations centers (SOCs) to maintain running estimates $(\bar{x}_k, s_k^2)$ using $O(1)$ memory. When an incoming network request arrives with score $X_k > \bar{x}_{k-1} + 3 s_{k-1}$, an automated intrusion alarm triggers instantly without recomputing historical logs.
